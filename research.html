Superstars have the power? Technological M&As and firm markups

[Working Paper] [Policy Brief version]

with E. Zaurino, M. Martinez Cillero, L. Napolitano, and F. Rentocchini · UNU-MERIT Working Paper No. 2026-017

Earlier version: M&As, Innovation and Superstar Firms, JRC Working Papers on Corporate R&D and Innovation No. 3/2025

Abstract

We study whether acquisitions of patent-holding firms (technological M&As) raise acquirer markups, exploiting the staggered timing of first technological acquisitions in a panel of 7,796 listed firms in 79 countries over 2009–2020. Technological M&As raise acquirer markups by 1.7 percent, roughly two years of growth in the aggregate US markup, with larger effects among top R&D investors. Post-acquisition patenting shows no detectable increase, and productivity declines. Markup gains increase with the size of the acquired patent stock and with technological proximity to the target, consistent with acquired patents shielding acquirers from rivals rather than with innovation or efficiency gains.

A composite indicator of General Purpose Technologies and their dynamics over the long run

[Draft]

Abstract

We construct a continuous composite indicator of general purposeness (GPness) using annual data on 3-digit US patent classes over 1920–2014. The benefit-of-the-doubt approach combines the three characteristics of a GPT — growth, innovation complementarity and use complementarity — into a measure of proximity to the yearly frontier. Varying weights accommodate technologies at different stages of maturity while requiring minimum contributions from every dimension. High GPness is rare: only 24 classes attain the frontier. The composition of leading technologies shifts from mechanical technologies towards chemicals and, subsequently, computers and communications, while electrical and electronic technologies remain prominent. Leadership at the top one per cent exhibits major breaks around 1961 and 1988, separating three relatively stable eras. Ascent to technological leadership is gradual and heterogeneous: four era-defining classes take fourteen to thirty-five years to progress from the top decile to the top one per cent. These findings connect individual technological trajectories to the succession of techno-economic paradigms and distinguish leadership from initial emergence.

Skills and AI Innovative Ecosystems: Evidence from Brazilian Microregions

[new draft coming soon]

with R. Consentino de la Vega, F. Jaccoud, M. Menendez, and T. Ciarli

Abstract

This paper proposes a data-driven approach to identify the skills most strongly associated with the emergence of AI firms across Brazilian functional labour market regions (Regiões Geográficas Imediatas, RGIs). We combine worker-level employment records from RAIS with the ESCO skills taxonomy and firm-level data from FactSet/Crunchbase to construct a novel region–skill dataset covering nearly 13,500 skills across 392 RGIs. Using canonical correspondence analysis (CCA), we extract the latent skill dimensions that maximally correlate with AI firm presence, conditioning on regional income, the stock of digital firms, and broader digital innovation ecosystem factors. Across different model specifications, the skills most distinctively associated with AI firm presence remain stable, comprising foundational digital and organisational capabilities spanning software architecture, distributed computing, DevOps, cybersecurity, and advanced data platforms. These skills are geographically rare and concentrate in a small number of highly diversified regions, primarily major urban centres such as São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Curitiba, and Brasília. These findings suggest that pre-existing digital human capital endowments shape the geography of AI entrepreneurship, and that the regional concentration of AI activity in Brazil reflects structural path dependencies rooted in accumulated skill bases.
